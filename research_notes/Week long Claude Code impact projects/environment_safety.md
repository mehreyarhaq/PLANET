# Week-long Claude Code "discovery loop" projects: environment, public safety and the physical world (Ontario/Toronto angle)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Method: web search only (no page fetches were possible in this sandbox; Firecrawl had no credits), so every claim rests on search-result snippets and the pages they index. Where a saturation rating rests on *absence* of search evidence rather than positive evidence, it is explicitly labelled "(absence-based)". Terms-of-service wording for Amazon, Kijiji and Meta was only seen via secondary sources; the primary text was not retrieved.

Ratings scale used below: **crowded** / **some competition** / **open niche** / **open (unverified)**.

## Candidate 1 — Undocumented legacy oil & gas wells in southwestern Ontario (petroleum well DB × LiDAR/DTM × historical topos × 1954 air photos)

### Takeaway
All the input layers are open or free for a U of T student (Petroleum Wells under OGL-Ontario, lidar DTM under OGL-Ontario, OCUL georeferenced historical topographic maps, U of T's georeferenced 1954 air photos), the province openly admits thousands of wells are unrecorded and says it is "trying to map" them, and the US DOE CATALOG consortium has already proven the historical-map + AI method — but no search surfaced anyone applying it to Ontario. The realistic one-week output is a ranked *candidate list* for the ministry and municipalities to field-verify, not a confirmed discovery; that is still newsworthy given Wheatley.

### Cited Findings

**(1) Has it been done?**
- Ontario has roughly 27,000 recorded wells; at least 50,000 are estimated to have been drilled since the mid-1800s, and "spotty record keeping means there could be tens of thousands of unrecorded and lost wells" — [The Narwhal explainer](https://thenarwhal.ca/ontario-old-oil-gas-wells-explainer/)
- A 2014 AAPG abstract on the Abandoned Works Program says the program began in 2005 and that of ~50,000 wells in SW Ontario about 27,000 are on record with the ministry; the ministry's own locating work is described as geochemical fingerprinting of leaking gases, not remote sensing — [Search and Discovery abstract](https://www.searchanddiscovery.com/abstracts/html/2014/90195eastern/abstracts/31.html)
- Brant County: MNR holds 269 well records for the county; ~100 are inactive and ~100 others have missing information — [CTV Kitchener](https://www.ctvnews.ca/kitchener/article/thousands-of-abandoned-oil-wells-could-pose-danger-to-ontario-landowners-including-brant-county/); county handout says many landowners are unaware of wells on their land — [Brant County handout (PDF)](https://www.brant.ca/media/c13fq50f/legacy-oil-and-gas-wells-handout.pdf)
- Coverage indexed at [Farmtario](https://farmtario.com/news/dealing-with-decommissioned-oil-and-gas-wells-on-your-farm/) and [Kingsville Times](https://kingsvilletimes.ca/?p=91839) repeats a 2022 estimate of ~6,000 additional wells with unknown locations and a 2025 ministry statement that there are likely more without records; the minister reportedly said after Wheatley the province is "trying to map abandoned oil and gas wells with new technology" (the exact origin of the 6,000 figure was not confirmed).
- Wheatley, Aug 26 2021: explosion destroyed two buildings and injured ~20; gas was first detected June 2021 and the area had been evacuated three times before — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6155137), [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/2021/08/27/chatham-kent-seeks-provincial-help-after-suspected-gas-blast-in-wheatley-ont/); the Globe reported methane and H2S from one of three long-abandoned wells as the likely cause but that the investigation "still isn't complete" — [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-where-are-ontarios-abandoned-gas-wells-and-what-risks-do-they-pose); an abandoned well was found near the blast site — [CTV Windsor](https://windsor.ctvnews.ca/abandoned-gas-well-found-near-site-of-wheatley-explosion-1.5642690). Chatham-Kent "pleaded unsuccessfully" with the province via FOI'd documents — [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-ontario-fire-chiefs-press-province-action-wheatley-gas-explosion)
- CBC Windsor already built an interactive map of every Ontario well recorded as abandoned or unknown-status in the Ontario Petroleum Data System (OPDS) — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.6160949). (This is a map of *known* wells; it does not attempt to find unrecorded ones.)
- US precedent: Lawrence Berkeley Lab/CATALOG trained a U-Net to read oil-well symbols on historical USGS topographic maps, flagged symbols >100 m from any documented well, found 1,301 potential undocumented wells across four counties in California and Oklahoma (>40,000 km²), verified 29 by satellite and 15 by field magnetic survey — [Berkeley Lab news](https://newscenter.lbl.gov/2024/12/04/ai-helps-researchers-dig-through-old-maps-to-find-lost-oil-and-gas-wells/); DOE briefing slides (July 2024) — [catalog.energy.gov PDF](https://catalog.energy.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/DOE-UOWP-Slides-July_2024.pdf); NETL describes ML on "historic records and citizen-scientist reports" — [NETL](https://netl.doe.gov/node/12447); DOE notes lidar and magnetometry as reconnaissance tools and says decisioning must scale 40–400× — [NETL slides PDF](https://netl.doe.gov/sites/default/files/netl-file/22RS-25_Viswanathan.pdf)
- Alberta precedent: Lin & Rolnick, NeurIPS 2021 CCAI workshop, semantic segmentation of satellite imagery to find undocumented wells — [Climate Change AI](https://www.climatechange.ai/papers/neurips2021/64); follow-on "Alberta Wells Dataset" (>200k wells) — [arXiv 2410.09032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.09032v1)
- Searches for "Ontario undocumented wells LiDAR / machine learning" returned only the Alberta and US work above and nothing Ontario-specific (absence-based). A June 2026 Waterloo item on a master's student doing geophysical leak monitoring of legacy wells is in northern BC, not Ontario — [UWaterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/science/news/science-matters-masters-student-monitors-pollution-abandoned)
- Ontario policy: 2023 Legacy Oil and Gas Wells Action Plan, $23.6M, including doubling the Abandoned Works Program to $6M over three years and $7.5M for municipal emergency preparedness — [CTV Windsor](https://windsor.ctvnews.ca/ontario-invests-23-6m-into-province-wide-strategy-addressing-abandoned-oil-and-gas-wells-1.6424966); December 2025 added $4M for SW Ontario municipalities — [ES&E Magazine](https://esemag.com/hazmat-remediation/ontario-invests-emergency-preparedness-legacy-oil-gas-wells/); ERO consultation notice — [ERO 019-9286](https://ero.ontario.ca/notice/019-9286)
- Abandoned Works Program: ministry visits site, ranks by risk, pays pre-qualified contractor; landowner becomes responsible if no operator can be identified; apply via gasandoilwells@ontario.ca — [ontario.ca AWP page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/abandoned-works-program); program has spent $29.5M to cap 415 wells (avg $71,084/well) — [CTV Windsor](https://www.ctvnews.ca/windsor/article/ontario-government-increasing-funding-to-locate-and-plug-old-and-inactive-oil-and-gas-wells/); 454 wells plugged per a later release — [MPP Lecce post](https://www.stephenleccempp.ca/post/ontario-taking-action-to-address-risks-posed-by-abandoned-oil-and-gas-wells)

- Western Libraries air photo app: 10,000+ listings, 8,000+ scans, 900+ georeferenced, with a 50-year copyright caution — [Western](https://www.lib.uwo.ca/madgic/airphotos.html); Waterloo's 1955 orthomosaics cost $10/photo for non-UW users — [UWaterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/library/geospatial/air-photos-digitization-project)
- Ontario Water Well Information System (water wells, not petroleum) is open quarterly (Access/shapefile, last updated Aug 27 2025) with acknowledged location inaccuracies — [data.ontario.ca](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/well-records)

**(4) Impact pathway / verifier**
- MNR Petroleum Operations Section (posrecords@ontario.ca, 519-873-4634) and the AWP intake (gasandoilwells@ontario.ca) — [ontario.ca AWP page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/abandoned-works-program)
- Municipalities already asking for help: Chatham-Kent — [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/2021/08/27/chatham-kent-seeks-provincial-help-after-suspected-gas-blast-in-wheatley-ont/); Brant County — [Brant County page](https://www.brant.ca/emergency-services-and-health-care/oil-and-gas-wells/); Ontario Association of Fire Chiefs has pressed the province — [OAFC](https://oafc.on.ca/node/7891), [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-ontario-fire-chiefs-press-province-action-wheatley-gas-explosion)
- Journalists already on the beat: The Narwhal (FOI-based investigation on security deposits) — [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/old-gas-wells-ontario/); CBC Windsor; Globe and Mail; CTV Windsor/Kitchener.

**(5) Legal/ethical**
- Landowners become responsible for plugging if no operator exists — [ontario.ca AWP page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/abandoned-works-program) — so publishing parcel-level candidates can impose cost and stigma on private owners.
- Officials warn against anyone investigating a suspected well themselves; report to Spills Action Centre — [Penticton Herald/CP](https://www.pentictonherald.ca/spare_news/article_ffa8914a-4047-5625-9b96-7c1729aab18c.html), [Brant County](https://www.brant.ca/emergency-services-and-health-care/oil-and-gas-wells/)
- OGSR Library reuse terms not confirmed (see Gaps); the provincial OPDS extract under OGL-Ontario avoids that issue.

### Inferences
- **Week-loop sketch.** Day 1: pull Petroleum Wells FGDB + OGSR CSV; build a merged well table; define AOI = Lambton, Chatham-Kent, Essex, Norfolk, Brant, Haldimand (plus Elgin/Oxford if time). Day 2: download OCUL 1:25,000/1:63,360 sheets for the AOI; run a symbol detector (template-matching or a small U-Net fine-tuned on the LBNL approach; laptop-scale because sheets are few hundred MB total) for oil-derrick/well symbols; georeference hits. Day 3: download Ontario DTM 1 km tiles over hits and over known well clusters; compute hillshade/local-relief; look for circular pads, berms, pits and access tracks; train a simple classifier on *known* wells as positives. Day 4: overlay 1954 air photos (U of T) for derricks/tank batteries; cross-check against the 269 Brant records and the ~100 with missing info. Day 5: scoring — map symbol or pad >100 m from any recorded well = candidate; rank by concordance across three layers and proximity to homes/schools (sensitive). Day 6: falsification pass — exclude water wells (WWIS), quarry/pit features, silos, drainage; re-run with perturbed thresholds. Day 7: write-up with per-candidate evidence cards; send to MNR and the relevant municipality before any publication.
- A one-week run plausibly yields dozens-to-hundreds of candidates in the Petrolia/Oil Springs/Bothwell/Wheatley belts because derricks were dense and well-mapped on 1906–1930s topos; the LBNL hit-rate (1,301 candidates → 44 verified by remote/field means) suggests only a minority will survive verification, so the deliverable must be framed as leads.
- **Most likely headline:** "U of T student's AI scan of century-old maps flags hundreds of possible unrecorded gas wells in southwestern Ontario — ministry says it will check" (Toronto Star / CBC Windsor / The Narwhal).
- **Saturation: open niche for Ontario (absence-based)**; crowded in the US (DOE CATALOG, LBNL, NETL) and Alberta (Lin & Rolnick). The method is proven elsewhere, which raises credibility and lowers novelty risk.

### Gaps
- No Office of the Fire Marshal / final cause report for Wheatley surfaced; the Globe said the investigation was incomplete.
- The Narwhal explainer snippet mentions a further Wheatley leak "in June 2025", but a direct search returned only June–October 2021 leaks; the 2025 event is unverified.
- NRCan HRDEM licence/coverage for the AOI was not searched this session (it is generally open under OGL-Canada, but unverified here).
- OGSR Library's operative Terms & Conditions clauses (redistribution of derived candidate lists) were not retrievable.
- Whether the ministry's "new technology" mapping effort already includes lidar/AI is unknown; a direct enquiry to the Petroleum Operations Section is the cheapest way to find out before investing the week.

## Candidate 2 — Historic hazardous land uses from georeferenced Goad plans × today's schools, child-care centres, parks, residences × Records of Site Condition

### Takeaway
The Goad inputs are unusually good for Toronto (U of T has georeferenced every out-of-copyright plan/atlas plate and they are downloadable), the Ontario child-care and school lists are open, and no search found any academic, journalistic or city project that systematically overlays Goad-era hazardous uses on today's sensitive sites — but the Records of Site Condition registry is per-site, not bulk, the province's contaminated-sites dataset is currently "under review", and the project carries real stigma/property-value risk that dictates reporting at the neighbourhood level, not the parcel level, unless a verifier confirms.

### Cited Findings

**(1) Has it been done?**
- International precedents: MIT/Boston Public Health Commission pilot overlaid Sanborn 1888 and 1962 industrial uses with demographic/health data, assigning hazardous chemicals by industry type (URISA Journal 2004) — [Esri ArcNews](https://esri.com/news/arcnews/summer03articles/boston-mass.html); Grand Rapids Sanborn-based environmental-justice thesis — [WMU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/masters_theses/5159); NYC Office of Environmental Remediation historic land use dataset on vacant manufacturing-zoned lots built from Sanborn maps — [data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/historic-land-use-data). No study linking fire-insurance-map contamination mapping to *school* sites was found (absence-based).
- Toronto precedents are site-specific, not systematic: TDSB ordered soil tests at Swansea PS after lead up to nine times provincial limits was found 20 cm down, attributed to 1940s industrial landfill with ash and cinders — [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/news/national/board-wants-lead-tests-of-soil-at-swansea-school/article4150966); South Riverdale's 1988 $10M lead-soil replacement at ~900 properties — [TPL Digital Archive](https://digitalarchive.tpl.ca/objects/372696/getting-lead-out), [Seven News 1984](https://www.Connexions.org/CxLibrary/CX5413.htm); Toronto Star's 2017 Jesse McLean investigation into Mississauga's arsenic/lead street-sweepings — [Toronto Star via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/canada/toronto-star/20170403/281513635999416)
- A 1998 "Historical Land Use Inventories: A Guide to Their Creation" (Canadian Urban Institute with the City of Toronto Environmental Protection Office) shows the City considered building such an inventory decades ago — [CCLM Portal](https://www.cclmportal.ca/resource/historical-land-use-inventories-guide-their-creation); a 1998 council report listed old landfill sites — [City of Toronto 1998](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/1998/agendas/committees/wu/wu980909/it019.htm)
- The City's own Phase 1 ESAs (e.g., Wabash Community Centre) use fire insurance plans, aerials, city directories and ERIS searches, but only per project — [City of Toronto PDF](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/9626-wabash-community-centre-executive-summary-enviro-site-assessment-phase-1.pdf), [2026 Phase 1 ESA](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/8068-RGMEAAppxG-Phase-1-Environmental-Site-Assessment.pdf)
- No Toronto Star "lead in Toronto soil" neighbourhood investigation surfaced in two searches (absence-based); the closest Star items are the 2017 Mississauga story and 2017/2019 lead-in-school-water work.

**(2) Data access**
- U of T MDL: "all out-of-copyright fire insurance plans and atlas plates of Toronto have been digitized and georeferenced (UTM NAD83 Zone 17N), and are available for download" via an ArcGIS Online Data Index — [MDL Fire Insurance Plans and Atlases](https://mdl-data.library.utoronto.ca/items/show/1813); a hosted Toronto1903 tile service covering downtown and the Islands exists (Georia Project) — [ArcGIS tile service](https://tiles.arcgis.com/tiles/jIL9msH9OI208GCb/arcgis/rest/services/Toronto1903/MapServer)
- Toronto Public Library holdings viewable online 1858–1912; Volumes 10 and 11 not digitized — [TPL](https://tpl.ca/history-genealogy/lh-toronto-fire-insurance-plans.jsp); Canadiana scans — [Canadiana](https://www.canadiana.ca/view/oocihm.25003)
- Records of Site Condition: governed by Part XV.1 EPA and O. Reg. 153/04 — [ontario.ca law](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/040153/v29); filed RSCs are public per-site documents (e.g., [Uxbridge RSC 225427](https://www.uxbridge.ca/en/business-and-development/resources/Documents/Bridgebrook-7370-Centre-Rd/Record-of-Site-Condition-225427.pdf), [Mississauga RSC B-403-3379300641, Oct 2025](https://www.mississauga.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/01122023/Rec-of-Site-Cond-OZ-OPA-25-9-W5-and-21T-M-25-2-W5-Oct-2025.pdf)); no bulk/open download of the registry was found in two searches (absence-based); ERIS sells per-site RSC packages commercially — [ERIS](https://www.erisinfo.com/products/ontario-rsc/)
- Ontario "Contaminated sites database / historical landfills database" on data.ontario.ca is listed but "not available right now because the data is under review" — [data.ontario.ca](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/contaminated-sites-databasehistorical-landfills-database)
- Licensed child care facilities in Ontario: open, updated monthly (May 2026 file) — [data.ontario.ca](https://data.ontario.ca/en/dataset/licensed-child-care-facilities-in-ontario); school board/private school contact lists are open — [data.ontario.ca](https://data2.ontario.ca/dataset/school-board-and-school-authority-contact-information/resource/d35fba1d-54fc-4250-b97e-3134a62d4c3a)

**(5) Legal/ethical**
- Tridan v. Shell (Ont. C.A.): "knowing a site was once polluted is not in itself stigma" after a pristine clean-up; trial judge had awarded stigma damages — [Dalhousie Journal of Legal Studies](https://digitalcommons.schulichlaw.dal.ca/djls/vol11/iss1/6), [Siskinds](https://www.siskinds.com/disclose-contamination/?amp=1)
- Research finds perceived risk alone can lower property values even without proven contamination — [MDPI Sustainability](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/12/5/2046); stigma persists mainly for houses very close to hazardous sites — [EPA economics paper](https://archive.epa.gov/ncer/publications/web/pdf/ee-0428-01.pdf)
- Non-disclosure case law (1623242 Ontario Inc. v. Great Lakes Copper, PCB contamination in Fergus) shows ministry orders can reach past and present owners — [Siskinds](https://www.siskinds.com/disclose-contamination/?amp=1)
- No source on defamation exposure for publishing historic-use overlays was found (gap); the risk is primarily property stigma and implying negligence by a school board or operator.

### Inferences
- **Week-loop sketch.** Day 1: download all georeferenced Goad sheets (1880/1884/1890/1899/1903/1910/1912/1913/1924) from MDL; build a gazetteer of hazardous-use keywords (gas works, tannery, foundry, dye works, lead works, paint, coal yard, scrap, dry cleaner, battery, plating, radium/luminous). Day 2–3: OCR/vision-pass each sheet (laptop-scale; a few hundred plates), extract labelled polygons; dedupe across editions; geocode to parcels. Day 4: overlay on current TDSB/TCDSB school sites, licensed child-care centres (open), City parks and residential parcels; compute buffer hits. Day 5: cross-check each hit against (a) RSC registry by address (scripted per-site lookups), (b) City Phase 1/2 ESA PDFs on toronto.ca, (c) the 1998 landfill list. Day 6: falsify — remove sites with known remediation, modern redevelopment under RSC, or where the historic use was small/short-lived; weight by use severity and duration. Day 7: produce a neighbourhood-level map plus a confidential parcel list for TDSB/Toronto Public Health/MECP.
- A finding is realistic in a week: the plates exist, the sensitive-site lists exist, and the comparison is deterministic. The scarce part is the RSC check (per-site, not bulk) — scriptable but slow; expect to cover hundreds, not thousands, of addresses.
- **Most likely headline:** "Dozens of Toronto schools and daycares sit on or beside century-old tanneries, gas works and foundries — and there's no public record they were ever tested" (Toronto Star / The Varsity).
- **Saturation: open niche (absence-based)** for the systematic overlay; "some competition" from consultants (ERIS/Phase 1 ESA industry) who do this per site for a fee and would be natural verifiers.
- Responsible reporting: publish aggregate/neighbourhood results; share parcel-level leads with TDSB, Toronto Public Health and MECP first; never assert contamination, only "historic use + no public record of assessment".

### Gaps
- Whether the Environmental Site Registry has a scriptable search (URL, query parameters, rate limits) could not be confirmed; only per-site PDFs were visible.
- No Toronto-specific "Port Lands / Junction / Leslieville" soil-study citations surfaced in these searches; those precedents remain unverified here.
- Defamation exposure under Ontario law for this kind of publication was not found in any source.

## Candidate 3 — Ontario drinking water: mass extraction of ~650 municipal annual/summary reports + DWSP + lead-in-schools data

### Takeaway
The regulatory data are already open and tabular (Drinking Water Quality and Enforcement fiscal-year zips with test results, adverse incidents, orders and convictions; DWSP 1998–2022), the lead-in-schools angle is crowded (Toronto Star 2017, Tainted Water 2019, IJB's Lead Tracker updated through 2025), and the non-municipal side was just audited by the Auditor General (March 2025). The remaining open niche is cross-checking the ~650 municipal O. Reg. 170/03 annual reports (scattered PDFs) against the ministry's own extract to find systems whose reports omit or understate adverse incidents.

### Cited Findings

**(1) Has it been done?**
- Tainted Water (2019): Concordia's Institute for Investigative Journalism with Global News, Toronto Star and others; 120+ journalists, 700+ FOIs; 33% of 12,000 tests in 11 cities exceeded Health Canada's lead guideline — [Global News "Inside the investigation"](https://globalnews.ca/news/6134683/inside-the-investigation-tainted-water-canada/amp), [Michener Awards](https://www.michenerawards.ca/organization/institute-for-investigative-journalism/)
- Toronto Star 2017: "More than 640 schools, daycares failed lead tests" — [PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/canada/toronto-star/20171006/281509341396852)
- IJB/U of T HIVE Lab School & Daycare Lead Tracker: >175,000 results 2019–2025; one in ten tests exceeded 5 ppb; more than half of public schools had at least one exceedance; >27% of child-care centres; Ontario does not require proactive parent notification — [IJB 2025 update](https://ijb.utoronto.ca/?p=10581), [IJB earlier](https://ijb.utoronto.ca/?p=4096); Ottawa schools named — [CTV Ottawa](https://ottawa.ctvnews.ca/schools-in-ottawa-identified-in-study-showing-high-levels-of-lead-in-drinking-water-1.6922640); Global 2020 on school water — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/6603235/ontario-schools-safe-water)
- Auditor General of Ontario special report, March 31 2025, non-municipal systems: >98% of samples met standards; 52% of 33 health units with small systems had not inspected all as required; ~34% of systems uninspected 5+ years; 56% of 1,660 systems missed at least one required sample in five years; 35% of private-well samples 2003–2022 showed bacterial indicators; ~3M Ontarians; 17 recommendations — [AG report PDF](https://auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en25/pa_drinkingwater_en25.pdf), [AG news release](https://auditor.on.ca/en/content/news/25_newsreleases/nr_AR_en25-02.pdf), [ES&E](https://esemag.com/water/ontario-audit-finds-gaps-in-inspections-compliance-for-small-non-municipal-drinking-water-systems/). Fine rate reported as 1% of 932 non-compliant systems by [Law360](https://www.law360.ca/ca/amp/articles/2318245) vs 11% in [Kenora coverage](https://yourkenora.ca/?p=14252) (conflict).
- 2024–25 Chief Drinking Water Inspector report (Steven Carrasco): 98.1% of ~100,000 small-system results met standards; AWQIs "decreased significantly over time" — [ontario.ca](https://www.ontario.ca/page/2024-2025-chief-drinking-water-inspector-annual-report); 2025 Minister's report: 8 orders to non-municipal residential owners, 6 to designated facilities, 3 to labs; 5 convictions totalling $87,500 — [ontario.ca](https://www.ontario.ca/page/2025-ministers-annual-report-drinking-water)
- Probe International critique: 35% of municipal systems failed at least one test, 58% had AWQIs (report year unclear) — [Probe International](https://environment.probeinternational.org/tag/ontario-chief-drinking-water-inspector/)

## Candidate 4 — Recalled products still for sale on Canadian online marketplaces (Health Canada recalls × Amazon.ca / Walmart.ca / Kijiji / Facebook Marketplace)

### Takeaway
The recall side is clean (official JSON API under OGL-Canada, updated daily) and the story has strong precedents abroad (PIRG, Consumer Reports, Which?, Electrical Safety First, US lawmakers vs Meta) and a 2025 Health Canada pledge progress report conceding sellers evade screening — but the marketplace side is contractually hostile: Amazon's Conditions of Use bar data mining/robots, Kijiji's Terms bar automated collection without written permission, and Meta's Automated Data Collection Terms (Oct 2024) require express written permission. A continuous week-long agent crawl is exactly the behaviour those terms prohibit; a defensible design is low-volume, manual-equivalent, logged-out searches of specific recalled model numbers, which caps scale.

## Candidate 5 — Methane / NO2 super-emitter attribution in Ontario with TROPOMI (+ Carbon Mapper, MethaneSAT) × NPRI, landfills, gas infrastructure

### Takeaway
This is the most crowded and least feasible candidate for an amateur: TROPOMI's 5.5 × 7 km pixels and ~10–25 t/h informal detection floor cannot attribute emissions to a single Ontario landfill or compressor station; MethaneSAT died in June 2025; Canadian landfill inventory science is already contested in both directions by funded groups (Carbon Mapper, GHGSat, ECCC, Waterloo, U of T's TAME/FLAME-GTA); and new federal Landfill Methane Regulations (Dec 2025) mean regulators are already instrumenting the sector. A narrower NO2 angle (Goldberg et al. found Toronto's NOx inventory 21% low) is academically owned.

### Cited Findings

**(1) Has it been done / how crowded?**
- TROPOMI ML super-emitter detection: 2,974 plumes in 2021, ~35% linked to urban areas/landfills — [FAO AGRIS record of Schuit et al.](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/669f679600eb85b7d72cb287), [Aben seminar PDF](https://www2.acom.ucar.edu/sites/default/files/2024-03/20240319_Ilse_Aben_ACOM_seminar.pdf); landfill super-emitters at Buenos Aires, Delhi, Lahore, Mumbai (3–29 t/h) — [ZME Science on Science Advances 2022](https://zmescience.com/science/scientists-use-satellites-to-uncover-large-methane-emissions-from-landfills-11082022); hyperspectral (EMIT/EnMAP/PRISMA) follow-up on 38 TROPOMI-flagged landfills: 230 ± 15 t/h, 1.8× Climate TRACE — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12409875/)
- Carbon Mapper airborne surveys covered large landfills in 18 US states and three Canadian provinces (2016–2022), with point sources at 52% of sites — [EGU23-9802](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU23/EGU23-9802.html); GHGSat reports landfill emissions "most prevalent in Ontario, Quebec and the eastern provinces" and that 9 of 13 sampled US landfills under-reported — [Waste Dive](https://www.wastedive.com/news/ghgsat-landfill-emissions-trend-report/742285/); GHGSat's Terrebonne, QC plume ~1.2 t/h (48% uncertainty), attributed by the operator to biogas-system work — [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-quebec-landfill-methane-leak-canadian-satellite/); ECCC's independent evaluation of GHGSat across Canada found a ~10% detection rate for landfill scenes — [McLinden et al. 2024 JGR (ECCC PDF)](https://eer.cmc.ec.gc.ca/publications/h63GpyD9s182fVbZak76uwq838ld/McLinden.et_al.2024.JGRA.vol129.no15.e2023JD039906.pdf)
- Canadian inventory science cuts the other way: a 2025 Elementa study of 42 Canadian landfills found ECCC's first-order-decay model *over*-predicts most measured rates — [Elementa via CiteDrive](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/canadas-landfill-methane-inventories-the-challenge-of-accurate-modeled-and-measurement-based-emissions/); an EGU24 mass-balance study also found the model often overestimates — [EGU24-9675](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU24/EGU24-9675.html); Waterloo runs a "Mitigation of Methane Emission Hot Spots" program for municipal landfills — [UWaterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/mitigation-methane-hotspots/about-mitigation-methane-emission-hot-spots-municipal)
- Toronto: FLAME-GTA bottom-up estimate 86 ± 38 Gg/yr vs EDGAR 96 ± 48 and ECCC 143 ± 71 (inventories higher, not lower) — [ACP 2019](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/19/3963/2019/acp-19-3963-2019-metrics.html); U of T TAME project continuing with 1 km WRF-GEOS-Chem (EGU 2026) — [EGU26-15301](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-15301.html?pdf), [U of T GTA-Emissions publications](https://www.atmosp.physics.utoronto.ca/GTA-Emissions/About/publications.html)
- NO2: Goldberg et al. 2019 derived TROPOMI top-down NOx for NYC, Chicago and Toronto and found Toronto's inventory 21% low — [Goldberg 2019 EST (PDF)](https://blogs.gwu.edu/sanenberg/files/2021/10/Goldberg_2019_EST.pdf); Zhao et al. 2020 assessed TROPOMI NO2 quality over the GTA — [NDACC](https://ndacc.larc.nasa.gov/publications/2020/assessment-quality-tropomi-high-spatial-resolution-no2-data-products-greater); no Hamilton/Sarnia TROPOMI study surfaced (absence-based).
- Environmental Defence Canada: no satellite-methane investigation found in two searches (absence-based); Canadian satellite stories so far are Alberta/Saskatchewan (Kayrros plume — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-05-20/methane-plume-detected-over-alberta-gas-field); Lloydminster 11 t/h — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/edmonton/methane-emissions-alberta-saskatchewan-1.6650755)) and Quebec landfills.

**(2) Data access and resolution**
