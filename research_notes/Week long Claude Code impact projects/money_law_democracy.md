## Candidate 1: Contract splitting and procurement anomalies in federal proactive-disclosure contract data (plus Ontario and Toronto)

### Takeaway
The federal contracts dataset is bulk-downloadable, large (~925,000 rows, ~400 MB CSV as of 2022) and already heavily mined by Sean Boots and Amanda Clarke (govcanadacontracts.ca, a 2024 peer-reviewed paper), the IJF (948,000 awards, daily updates), the Auditor General and the Procurement Ombud; but none of the sources found describe a published, comprehensive algorithmic sweep for threshold-hugging, serial amendments or same-vendor splitting across all departments. Rating: **some competition on the data, open niche on the specific splitting sweep (unverified)**.

### Cited Findings
**Has it been done?**
- Sean Boots built govcanadacontracts.ca from the proactive-disclosure dataset ("925,000 entries in a 400MB CSV file"), breaking contracts down by vendor, department and category; he estimated federal IT contract spending at about $4.6 billion in the most recent fiscal year — [sboots.ca, 2022](https://sboots.ca/2022/08/31/how-much-does-the-canadian-government-spend-on-it-contracts-each-year/)
- Boots and Prof. Amanda Clarke (Carleton) published "Breaking All the Rules: Information Technology Procurement in the Government of Canada" in Canadian Public Administration (open access, Oct 2024), arguing federal practice departs from best practice on contract values and lengths, supplier diversity, sourcing of expertise and IP; the tool covers federal contracts 2017–2022 and focuses on IT — [sboots.ca, 2024](https://sboots.ca/2024/10/03/published-in-canadian-public-administration-breaking-all-the-rules-information-technology-procurement-in-the-government-of-canada/); [OGGO committee evidence](https://www.ourcommons.ca/documentviewer/en/44-1/OGGO/meeting-48/evidence); [OGGO evidence PDF](https://noscommunes.ca/Content/Committee/441/OGGO/Evidence/EV13228370/OGGOEV133-E.PDF)
- Auditor General Report 4 (June 10, 2025) on GC Strategies: 106 contracts from 31 federal organizations, April 2015–March 2024, maximum combined value $92.7 million, about $64.5 million paid; in 21% of contracts examined no documentation of valid security clearances; in 33% no evidence that resources had required qualifications; three confirming orders at TBS for work done without a contract; GC Strategies was 0.37% of informatics-services spending; the report deliberately contains no recommendations ("I don't believe the government needs more procurement rules") — [OAG report page](https://www.oag-bvg.gc.ca/internet/English/parl_oag_202506_04_e_44645.html); [OAG news release](https://www.oag-bvg.gc.ca/internet/English/mr_20250610_e_44662.html)
- Procurement Ombud 2024–25 annual report (tabled Oct 7, 2025) lists a "Top 10 issues" and 11 follow-up reviews — [OPO 2024-25 annual report](https://opo-boa.gc.ca/rapports-reports/2024-2025/index-eng.html); the 2023–24 report highlighted "favouritism towards specific bidders ... the lack of documentation and gaping holes in the quality of contract information made public by departments" — [OPO news release, Oct 2024](https://www.canada.ca/en/procurement-ombudsman/news/2024/10/the-procurement-ombuds-2023-24-annual-report-stresses-the-need-for-immediate-action.html)
- OPO released a "bait and switch" review of professional-services contracts in October 2025 — [OPO news release, Oct 2025](https://www.canada.ca/en/procurement-ombudsman/news/2025/10/procurement-ombud-releases-findings-of-his-review-of-bait-and-switch-in-professional-services-contracts-the-good-the-bad-and-the-unintended.html); [Law360 Canada](https://www.law360.ca/ca/articles/2400650)
- OPO's 2016–17 review of non-competitive contracting defines contract splitting as dividing a requirement that exceeds a delegated limit into several contracts, or issuing a contract under the limit and then amending it; "OPO has heard from suppliers that federal organizations may be manipulating requirements to avoid soliciting bids" — [OPO 2016-17 review (archived PDF)](https://webarchiveweb.wayback.bac-lac.canada.ca/web/20170823145356/http://opo-boa.gc.ca/documents/praapp-prorev/2016-2017/ecnc-rncc-eng.pdf)
- OPO's November 2021 Natural Resources Canada practice review has a section headed "Perception of contract splitting" (contents not visible in snippets) — [OPO NRCan review](https://opo-boa.gc.ca/praapp-prorev/2021/epa-pprn-11-2021-eng.html)
- PSPC assessor guidance item "12. Contract splitting" flags "multiple contracts or amendments to contracts against a single requirement or back-to-back contracts to the same supplier" and gives the example of multiple contracts for similar work "in order to invoke the 'does not exceed $40k' rule" — [PSPC assessor guidance](https://tpsgc-pwgsc.gc.ca/app-acq/spc-cps/sspc-cpss-doe-agd-p12-eng.html); a PSPC approval form restates the prohibition on splitting contracts or amendments to avoid statutory, TB or internal approvals or trade-agreement obligations — [PSPC approval form](https://tpsgc-pwgsc.gc.ca/app-acq/sp-ps/clients/approbation-approval-eng.html)
- The Directive on the Management of Procurement took effect May 13, 2021 — [Contracting Policy Notice 2021-3](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/policy-notice/contracting-policy-notice-2021-3-new-directive-management-procurement.html); Contracting Policy Notice 2026-1 raised most contracting limits in Appendix A for inflation, so thresholds used in a sweep must be checked against the current Appendix A — [CPN 2026-1](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/policy-notice/2026-1.html)
- The IJF's procurement database covers federal procurement plus BC, Vancouver and Victoria, with "more than 177,000 government tenders and 948,000 awards", daily updates, an advanced filter by contract method (competitive/non-competitive) and alerts; the full dataset requires a subscription — [IJF explore procurement](https://theijf.org/explore-procurement); [IJF launch post](https://theijf.org/ijf-launches-procurement-database)
- A vendor (Bounteous) describes a May 2026 hackathon prototype built on open federal and provincial procurement data that flags "contract amendment creep and vendor concentration"; no independent evaluation found — [Bounteous, May 2026](https://www.bounteous.com/insights/2026/05/20/what-canadas-public-sector-can-learn-agentic-ai-practice/)
- A third-party site summarises the proactive-publication data as about 244,187 non-competitive contracts worth $176.7 billion, roughly 18.6% of disclosed contracts by count (unofficial figures) — [canadasunshinelist.ca](https://www.canadasunshinelist.ca/blog/what-is-a-sole-source-non-competitive-federal-contract)
- Toronto: a Toronto Life piece says a Toronto Water audit found staff "in the habit of splitting purchase orders so they don't have to seek out competitive bids" (secondary, undated) — [Toronto Life](https://torontolife.com/city/i-wouldn%e2%80%99t-mind-an-increase-in-my-water-bill%e2%80%a6/); the Toronto Auditor General reviewed Divisional Purchase Orders after the limit rose to $50,000, concluding controls improved but could be strengthened — [Toronto AG purchasing reports index](https://www.torontoauditor.ca/reports/auditee/purchasing-and-materials-management)

**Data access**
- Dataset "Proactive Publication – Contracts" (TBS) on open.canada.ca offers a "Contracts over $10,000" CSV, a "Nothing to Report" CSV, an "Aggregated Total – Contracts $10K and under" CSV and a legacy-data file — [open.canada.ca dataset d8f85d91](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/d8f85d91-7dec-4fd1-8055-483b77225d8b)
- Publication rules: any contract over $10,000 and any amendment that changes value by more than $10,000; values include taxes; published quarterly under ss. 77 and 86 of the Access to Information Act, so a contract "could be proactively published up to four to five months after the contract was awarded"; for task-authorization contracts the published value may be below the true maximum — [search.open.canada.ca contracts](https://search.open.canada.ca/contracts/)
- Contracts and amendments of $10,000 or less are disclosed only annually as volume and cumulative value (Directive Appendix C.2.4.2), so splitting below $10k is invisible in the row-level data — [TBS archived guidelines (2020)](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32759); [Guide to the Proactive Publication of Contracts](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32763)
- A mirror of the dataset states the data is unaudited, "no warranty or other legal assurance as to the accuracy or completeness", and its data-dictionary field is empty — [donnees.iriu.ca mirror](https://donnees.iriu.ca/en/dataset/289138dc-2a51-4a5c-a461-974135d65e80)
- Toronto: awards from open competitive solicitations above $133,800 are published on the Toronto Bids Portal (supplier, total amount excluding option years, division, award date); a 2020 PMMD routine-disclosure plan lists competitive award results and non-competitive procurements as routinely available — [Toronto 2025 background file](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2025/gg/bgrd/backgroundfile-256906.pdf); [PMMD Routine Disclosure Plan](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/9717-PMMD-Routine-Disclosure-Plan.pdf)
- Ontario: "Public Accounts: Detailed Schedule of Payments" (formerly Volume 3) is on data.ontario.ca as annual CSVs (2016-17 through 2022-23 listed) of ministry payments to vendors and transfer-payment recipients, but it is explicitly not under the Open Government Licence – Ontario (ontario.ca terms of use apply) — [data.ontario.ca resource](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/public-accounts-detailed-schedule-of-payments/resource/484d4aa0-3070-4930-a88a-967ebf14f444)

**Legal risk**
- The Supreme Court's responsible-communication defence (Grant v Torstar, 2009 SCC 61) protects publication on matters of public interest where the publisher was diligent in verifying, considering seriousness, source reliability and whether the subject's side was sought; it applies to all communicators, not only journalists — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_v_Torstar_Corp); [Osgoode TheCourt](https://www.yorku.ca/osgoode/thecourt/2010/01/11/grant-v-torstar-corp-responsible-communication-on-matters-of-public-interest/)

### Inferences
- Week-feasibility (laptop-scale): Day 1 download the full contracts CSV (+ legacy file) and build a vendor-name normaliser (the data is unaudited and vendor strings are inconsistent). Day 2 compute, per department and vendor, counts of contracts within 2–5% below each Appendix A threshold (sole-source $40k, departmental limits), windows of 30/90 days between same-vendor awards, and amendment chains (original value vs. cumulative amendments). Day 3 run falsification tests: compare threshold-hugging density against a smooth baseline (bunching estimator), permutation tests across vendors, and check whether clusters disappear once task-authorization contracts are excluded. Day 4 pull the originals via search.open.canada.ca for the top 50 flags and have the agent read each record (solicitation procedure, limited-tendering reason). Day 5 cross-reference flagged vendors against OAG/OPO reports to separate "already known" from new. Day 6 re-run with the quarterly refresh and write up with per-flag provenance. Day 7 buffer. A quantitative finding (e.g., "N departments show statistically significant bunching just below $40k; vendor X received M contracts in 60 days each under the limit") is realistic in a week because the data is one flat file; the risk is that bunching reflects legitimate standing-offer call-ups rather than evasion.
- Impact pathway and verifier: the Office of the Procurement Ombud (which explicitly audits "contract splitting" and runs follow-up reviews) and the OAG; the OGGO committee has already hosted Boots/Clarke on exactly this dataset. Likely headline: "Federal contracts cluster just under the $40,000 no-bid line, data analysis finds" (Globe/CBC/The Logic). A Toronto angle ("City divisions splitting purchase orders") is harder because Toronto only publishes awards above $133,800 and the DPO data is not an open dataset.
- Legal/ethical risk: naming vendors as "splitters" implies wrongdoing by public servants and vendors; the safe framing is department-level statistics plus named examples drawn verbatim from official records, with comment sought. Responsible-communication defence applies to non-journalists, but verification diligence must be documented.
- Saturation: the data is crowded (Boots/Clarke, IJF, OAG, OPO, Bounteous prototype), but no published all-departments threshold-bunching or serial-amendment sweep surfaced in any search. Rating: open niche (unverified).

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the CSV field names (solicitation_procedure, limited_tendering_reason, original_value, amendment_value, number_of_bids): the data-dictionary search returned only CanadaBuys tender pages; verify by downloading the file header.
- The OPO NRCan review's actual finding on "perception of contract splitting" was not retrievable (search budget exhausted before the follow-up ran).
- No search found a Globe and Mail, CBC, Toronto Star or The Logic story explicitly analysing threshold-hugging in the proactive-disclosure data (absence of evidence only).
- Could not verify whether the current Directive text contains an explicit "contract splitting" clause; the prohibition is confirmed only via PSPC guidance that quotes older TB policy.
- Toronto DPO-level data and the Ontario Tenders Portal awards were not confirmed as bulk-downloadable.

## Candidate 2: Straw-donor / funnelled-contribution detection in Elections Canada and Elections Ontario data

### Takeaway
Bulk contribution data exists at both levels (federal CSV from 2004; Elections Ontario bulk-download utility from 2014, $200.01 aggregate threshold), and descriptive "who donates" work is crowded (IJF all-jurisdiction database, Postmedia, Democracy Watch, Narwhal/Star Greenbelt analysis); but no source found describes a systematic, data-driven straw-donor detector in Canada, and enforcement precedents (SNC-Lavalin 2016, Axor 2019) came from investigations, not data sweeps. Rating: **crowded for descriptive analysis; open niche for algorithmic funnelling detection (unverified)**, with a high defamation and false-positive risk because public data lacks employer fields.

### Cited Findings
**Data access**
- Elections Canada publishes "Contribution details of all political entities from January 1, 2004, to the present as reviewed by Elections Canada" as CSV zips (od_cntrbtn_audt_e.zip / _f.zip), noting it "may include updates to the original return by the agent and minor corrections made by Elections Canada" — [open.canada.ca dataset ef1e3528](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/ef1e3528-b570-4a42-92ef-18a9749af8f2); [Elections Canada open data index](https://www.elections.ca/content.aspx?section=fin&dir=oda&document=index&lang=e); an as-filed variant also exists — [open.canada.ca dataset 6495ea57](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/6495ea57-3824-48f0-a2b2-88477122a286)
- Elections Ontario's CFO handbooks (January 2025) say the website offers "a bulk download utility for contributions and a searchable political financing database" — [CFO Handbook for Candidates](https://prod-www.elections.on.ca/content/dam/NGW/sitecontent/Compliance%20Documentation/English/Candidates/CFO%20Handbook%20for%20Candidates.pdf); the public application covers 2014 to present, shows contributions for a unique donor with aggregate total of $200.01 or more (pre-2021 threshold $100.01), and pre-2014 records are hard copy at $1.00 per page — [Elections Ontario FAQ](https://elections.on.ca/en/voting-in-ontario/frequently-asked-questions/election-finances-reporting-system.html); [Elections Ontario financial statements](https://www.elections.on.ca/en/political-financing0/financial-statements.html)
- The IJF "Political Donors" database covers all donations at the federal level and in every province and territory from 1993 to present, filterable by donor name, recipient, location, amount, party and donor type; Quebec publishes name, postal code and city of every contributor; Saskatchewan allows anonymous donations up to $250 — [IJF explore donations](https://theijf.org/explore-donations); a Mitacs project "Follow the money: investigating political donations in Canada" supports it — [Mitacs](https://www.mitacs.ca/fr-ca/nos-projets/follow-the-money-investigating-political-donations-in-canada/)
- Postmedia hosts a Qlik searchable database of federal and provincial contributions (web tool, not bulk) — [Who backs Canada's politicians](https://webapps.qlik.com/np-donations/index.html)
- Democracy Watch published an analysis of Ontario party donations by amount for 2021–2022 — [Democracy Watch PDF](https://democracywatch.ca/wp-content/uploads/AnalysisOfOntPartyDonationsByAmounts2021-2022.pdf)

**Precedents and prior analyses**
- SNC-Lavalin's 2016 compliance agreement: executives solicited staff donations, reimbursed via "refunds for personal expenses or fictitious bonuses or other benefits", totalling $117,803.49; parties repaid the Receiver General — [Canada Gazette, Sept 2016](https://canadagazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2016/2016-09-10/html/parliament-parlement-eng.html); one former executive was later charged — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.4666929)
- 2019: Groupe AXOR and Axor Experts-Conseils reimbursed employees' donations; penalties equal to three times the donations plus investigative costs ($248,712 and $199,163), the first use of monetary-penalty powers from the 2018 amendments — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/5830954/montreal-companies-election-donations); [CFJC Today](https://cfjctoday.com/?p=778019)
- Quebec: the DGEQ ordered the PQ to repay more than $264,000 in allegedly illegal 2006–2009 contributions; the party offered $90,000 — [CTV Montreal](https://montreal.ctvnews.ca/pq-to-pay-back-90-000-in-illegal-contributions-1.3198137); Quebec's limit is now $100 a year and reimbursement is explicitly barred — [CTV Montreal, 2024](https://montreal.ctvnews.ca/parti-quebecois-find-caq-political-financing-no-laughing-matter-in-saint-louis-du-ha-ha-1.6815342)
- Narwhal/Toronto Star Greenbelt investigation: nine developers' names, companies, families and senior staff matched $572,000 in Ontario PC donations since 2014 (De Gasperis-linked $294,000; Baldassarra $94,000), with the explicit caveat that reporters "cannot independently verify that the names refer to the same people" — [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/ford-ontario-greenbelt-cuts-developers/)
- CBC reported that all four board members of FH Health made maximum donations to the Ontario PCs within days, with related donations from wives, a brother-in-law and colleagues — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.6329691)
- Global News (2025) raised questions about donations linked to Ontario Skills Development Fund recipients — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11499290/ontario-skills-development-fund-donations-questions/amp)
- Municipal: Campaign Fairness and York University's Robert MacDermid found the development industry was "by far the most important financier" of winning candidates in GTA municipalities except Toronto and Ajax; ACORN alleged 34% of 2018 Toronto contributions came from development-linked donors, a methodology councillors disputed; Toronto banned corporate/union donations before 2010 — [CTV Toronto](https://toronto.ctvnews.ca/activists-say-ontario-developers-have-too-much-sway-in-local-elections-1.2053118); [ACORN](https://acorncanada.org/blogto-all-toronto-city-council-alleged-have-accepted-donations-developers/); CBC Ottawa ran a developer-donations analysis — [CBC Ottawa](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/ottawa/developer-donations-analysis-ottawa-city-hall-1.4833045)

**Legal risk**
- Responsible communication defence (Grant v Torstar) requires diligence proportional to the seriousness of the allegation and seeking the subject's side — [Osgoode TheCourt](https://www.yorku.ca/osgoode/thecourt/2010/01/11/grant-v-torstar-corp-responsible-communication-on-matters-of-public-interest/)

### Inferences
- Week-feasibility: Day 1 download federal CSV and Elections Ontario bulk file; normalise names, cities, postal codes. Day 2 build candidate clusters: same surname + same postal code/city giving to the same recipient within 7 days at or near the annual limit; identical amounts on identical dates from 4+ donors sharing a postal code. Day 3 link clusters to corporate directorships via the Corporations Canada API / Ontario registry (directors are not in the federal bulk file; see Candidate 3) to test the "same employer/board" hypothesis. Day 4 falsify: compare against a null model of household giving (spouses legitimately giving maximum each), fundraising-event dates (many donors same day is normal), and riding-level base rates. Day 5 rank the few clusters that survive and check for existing compliance agreements. Day 6 write up as patterns with the "we cannot confirm identity" caveat used by Narwhal/Star. A finding of the form "N clusters of 5+ maximum donations from one address/company within a week" is realistic; proving reimbursement is not possible from public data.
- Impact pathway and verifier: the Commissioner of Canada Elections (compliance agreements and administrative monetary penalties) federally; Elections Ontario's Compliance Division provincially. Likely headline: "Clusters of maximum donations from the same households and boards flagged in Ontario PC fundraising data" (Star/Trillium/CBC).
- Legal/ethical risk: high. Naming individual donors as suspected straw donors is a serious allegation; false positives (families, fundraising dinners) are common; the public data omit employer, so "employer clustering" requires inference from other registries. Safer outputs are referrals to the Commissioner and aggregate, anonymised statistics.
- Saturation: crowded for descriptive databases (IJF covers every jurisdiction; Postmedia; Democracy Watch) and for developer-donor stories (Narwhal/Star, CBC, Global); open niche for a reproducible funnelling detector, but the rating rests on absence of search evidence.

### Gaps
- Field list of the Elections Canada CSV (whether postal code or full address is included, and confirmation that employer is absent) could not be confirmed from snippets; a vendor summary lists "contributor type, contribution range, election year, candidate information" only — [iWave](https://www.iwave.com/features/iwave-data/elections-canada/).
- Elections Ontario's public fields (name, city, postal code?) were not verified; the planned search did not run.
- No 2024–2026 compliance agreements involving reimbursed contributions surfaced; the Commissioner's own compliance-agreement list was not reachable.
- No search found a systematic academic or journalistic straw-donor detection study for Canada (absence of evidence).

## Candidate 3: Quality audit of Canada's federal beneficial-ownership (ISC) register

### Takeaway
Public ISC information has existed since January 22, 2024, but every source found says there is no public bulk file or API for ISC data (Open Ownership: structured data "No", API "No"), the official search works only by corporation name or number, and the federal bulk corporations file reportedly excludes even director names; the FATF/APG 2026 mutual evaluation already documents uneven provincial implementation. Rating: **open (unverified) on analysis, but blocked by data access**; a week-long loop would spend most of its time on per-corporation lookups of uncertain legality.

### Cited Findings
- Since January 22, 2024, CBCA corporations must file ISC information with Corporations Canada and "some of the ISC information will be made available to the public"; address-for-service may be used instead of residential address — [ISED ISC page](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/individuals-significant-control); [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2024/public-access-to-isc-information-under-the-cbca); public access began January 22, 2024 per Osler's comparison table — [Osler](https://www.osler.com/?p=28521)
- Open Ownership's country page records Canada's register as structured data publicly available: "No"; available via API: "No" — [Open Ownership Canada](https://www.openownership.org/en/map/country/canada/)
- Corporations Canada's data services describe a downloadable Federal Corporations dataset (CBCA corporations, not-for-profits, cooperatives, boards of trade) and a real-time API "including their status, registered office address, directors and many more" — [ISED data services](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/node/269); monthly transactions list only the last month online — [ISED monthly transactions](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/data-services/monthly-transactions); the Federal Corporations dataset is listed under the Open Government Licence — [open data mirror](https://data.amerigeoss.org/it/dataset/0032ce54-c5dd-4b66-99a0-320a7b5e99f2)
- A third-party developer post says the bulk file includes registered-office addresses but "Director names are not in the bulk data (only min/max counts)" — [dev.to (truenorthdata)](https://dev.to/truenorthdata/how-to-get-fresh-canadian-company-leads-by-api-2026-2d3i)
- Official search tips describe only two methods: by corporation number and by corporate name; trade names are not included — [ISED search tips](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/node/38)
- Sources conflict on bulk availability: Zephira.ai claims the ISC registry and an "open bulk dataset" are free; Global Database says bulk downloads exist only for Quebec via Données Québec — [Zephira.ai](https://zephira.ai/canada-company-registry-how-to-search-corporations-canada-in-2026/); [Global Database](https://www.globaldatabase.com/inside-our-mission-to-source-company-data-across-all-13-canadian-jurisdictions)
- Penalties for non-compliance: up to $100,000 and/or six months' imprisonment; reporting issuers and Crown corporations are exempt — [Dickinson Wright](https://www.dickinson-wright.com/news-alerts/new-filing-requirements-for-canadian-federal-corporations); [North Shore Law](https://www.northshorelaw.com/new-public-reporting-requirements-for-federal-business-corporations/)
- FATF/APG 2026 mutual evaluation: Canada "has considerably advanced its framework for beneficial ownership transparency", but effectiveness is held back by uneven implementation across provinces and territories, including limited central BO registries; it recommends continued work toward a pan-Canadian framework — [FATF MER 2026](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/canada-mer-2026.html); [copy hosted by canadiancharitylaw.ca](https://www.canadiancharitylaw.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FATF-Mutual-Evaluation-of-Canada-2026.pdf)
- Provincial picture (vendor summaries, to verify): Quebec's Enterprise Register discloses beneficial ownership publicly; Ontario private corporations keep internal registers since 2023 and the 2026 Ontario Budget reportedly targets a public registry in 2027; Alberta has no transparency-register legislation — [TMP Corp](https://tmpcorp.com/ca/blogs/beneficial-ownership-reporting-canada-2025/); [Zavia.ai](https://zavia.ai/how-to-verify-a-ubo-in-canada-2026-the-complete-14-jurisdiction-guide/); [Kyckr](https://kyckr.com/guides-and-reports/canada-business-registry-search-guide-2025)
- Privacy: a vendor guide says PIPEDA and Quebec's Law 25 restrict third-party processing and retention of personal information from registry records — [Business Data Guide](https://www.businessdataguide.com/blog/jurisdictions/canada-company-search-guide)
- Advocacy background: TI Canada has pushed for a public pan-Canadian registry since 2016; the 2021 Summit for Democracy commitment and the 2022 OGP action plan accelerated it — [TI Canada press release](https://transparencycanada.ca/news/press-release-transparency-international-canada-calls-on-ottawa-to-introduce-a-public-registry-of-canadian-registered-companies); [OGP story](https://www.opengovpartnership.org/stories/snow-washing-and-home-stashing-beneficial-ownership-transparency-in-canada/); earlier TI Canada ED comments that the register "will not be fully publicly available" — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.4427271)
- OpenCorporates offers sample bulk data for Corporations Canada with paid API for full data — [OpenCorporates register 44](https://opencorporates.com/registers/44)

### Inferences
- Week-feasibility: without a bulk ISC file the loop would have to (a) download the Federal Corporations bulk file (~hundreds of thousands of active CBCA corporations), (b) query the API/registry per corporation for ISC records, and (c) match names against OFAC/UK/EU/Canadian SEMA sanctions lists and PEP lists. Step (b) is the bottleneck: rate limits and terms are unknown, and bulk programmatic querying of a registry that Open Ownership records as having no API is likely to breach terms or be throttled. A one-week loop could realistically sample a few thousand corporations (e.g., all federal corporations in Toronto postal codes with "numbered company" names) and report filing-completeness statistics and impossible entries (dates of birth implying age < 18 or > 110, "address for service" = registered agent for hundreds of entities). Sanctions matches would need manual verification and would be rare.

## Adjacent idea B: Compliance audit of proactive-publication obligations themselves (late, missing and malformed contract disclosures)

### Takeaway
The Procurement Ombud has complained of "gaping holes in the quality of contract information made public by departments", the dataset is unaudited with an empty data dictionary, contracts must be published quarterly under ss. 77 and 86 of the Access to Information Act, and "Nothing to Report" files exist per department; an agent can measure each department's lateness, missing fields and implausible values without alleging wrongdoing by any vendor. Rating: **open niche (unverified)**.

### Cited Findings
- Quarterly publication is required by ss. 77 and 86 of Part 2 of the Access to Information Act; lag can be four to five months — [search.open.canada.ca contracts](https://search.open.canada.ca/contracts/)
- The dataset includes per-department "Nothing to Report" and aggregated small-contract files — [open.canada.ca dataset](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/d8f85d91-7dec-4fd1-8055-483b77225d8b); it is unaudited with no accuracy warranty — [mirror listing](https://donnees.iriu.ca/en/dataset/289138dc-2a51-4a5c-a461-974135d65e80)
- OPO 2023–24: "gaping holes in the quality of contract information made public by departments" — [OPO release](https://www.canada.ca/en/procurement-ombudsman/news/2024/10/the-procurement-ombuds-2023-24-annual-report-stresses-the-need-for-immediate-action.html)
- OAG 2025 found departments could not document clearances (21%) or qualifications (33%) in GC Strategies contracts, showing record-keeping failures are auditable — [OAG](https://www.oag-bvg.gc.ca/internet/English/parl_oag_202506_04_e_44645.html)

### Inferences
- Week loop: compute per-department publication lag (contract date vs. quarter published), share of rows with missing solicitation procedure or vendor, duplicate rows, amendments without a parent contract, and departments filing "Nothing to Report" while appearing as buyers in CanadaBuys awards. Verifier: TBS (policy owner), the Information Commissioner (ATIA Part 2) and OPO. Headline: "One in N federal contract disclosures is late or incomplete, breaching the Access to Information Act's transparency rules." Low legal risk (institutions only). Complements Candidate 1 and can share its pipeline.
