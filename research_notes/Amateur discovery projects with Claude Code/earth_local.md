## Key question 1: LiDAR archaeology in Ontario/Canada — open data, amateur precedents, AI site detection elsewhere, and legal/ethical rules

### Takeaway
Ontario and Canada publish bare-earth LiDAR-derived DTMs and classified point clouds as open data in 1 km tiles, and the UK/Netherlands/Ireland have strong precedents for amateurs and citizen-plus-AI projects finding undocumented features in exactly this kind of data, but no amateur or academic LiDAR archaeology find in Ontario surfaced in any search — the niche looks open, with the hard constraint that Ontario site locations are legally confidential and any fieldwork requires a licence.

### Cited Findings
**Open LiDAR/DEM data for Ontario and Toronto**
- The Ontario Digital Terrain Model (Lidar-Derived) is a bare-earth raster built from classified lidar point clouds, delivered as 1 km x 1 km non-overlapping tiles grouped into download packages; it compiles multiple acquisition projects so specs and sensors vary by project; the catalogue lists a data range of 2014-01-01 to 2019-01-01 and the Open Government Licence – Ontario — [Ontario Data Catalogue](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/ontario-digital-terrain-model-lidar-derived); mirrored at [open.canada.ca](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/7c3d7022-2631-45bc-8f6b-f3d51b7779a7)
- A tile index and a project-extents file are published as direct ZIP downloads: [OntarioDTM_LidarDerived_TileIndex.zip](https://www.publicdocs.mnr.gov.on.ca/mirb/OntarioDTM_LidarDerived_TileIndex.zip) and [OntarioLidarProjectExtents.zip](https://www.publicdocs.mnr.gov.on.ca/mirb/OntarioLidarProjectExtents.zip) — listed on [open.canada.ca](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/7c3d7022-2631-45bc-8f6b-f3d51b7779a7)
- The Ontario Classified Point Cloud (Lidar-Derived) is structured as 1 km x 1 km tiles in LAZ format with a listed date range of 2009–2019 — [Ontario Data Catalogue](https://data2.ontario.ca/dataset/ontario-classified-point-cloud-lidar-derived)
- The City of Toronto's own 2015 LiDAR (point cloud, 1 m grids, 50 cm contours, April 2015) is a licensed, not open, dataset: "provided for personal use for academic, research, and/or teaching purposes" with a data release agreement required — [University of Waterloo Geospatial Centre](https://uwaterloo.ca/lib-geospatial/collections/canadian-geospatial-data-resources/city-toronto-and-greater-toronto-area-gta/city-toronto-lidar-2015)
- NRCan's High Resolution Digital Elevation Model (HRDEM, CanElevation series) provides a bare-earth DTM and a surface model derived from airborne LiDAR in the south at 1 m or 2 m resolution; over 709,000 km² of lidar-derived DEMs have been added to HRDEM and HRDEM Mosaic since May 2024; projects are not edge-matched ("there is no integration or edgematching done between projects"); the NRCan page shows a point cloud of downtown Toronto including the CN Tower — [NRCan](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/science-data/science-research/geomatics/more-high-resolution-lidar-elevation-data-now-available); product spec: [HRDEM Product Specification PDF](https://download-telecharger.services.geo.ca/pub/elevation/dem_mne/highresolution_hauteresolution/HRDEM_Product_Specification.pdf)
- OpenTopography hosts the 1 m lidar-derived portion of Canada's HRDEM on demand, free to academic users — [OpenTopography](https://opentopography.org/node/3589)
- The older LEAP 2009 1 m DTM covers 4,567 km² around Prince Edward County (eastern Ontario) — [University of Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/lib-geospatial/collections/canadian-geospatial-data-resources/ontario/lidar-eastern-acquisition-project-leap-digital-terrain-model)

**Amateur LiDAR archaeology precedents (none found in Ontario; strong ones in UK/NL)**
- Searches for Ontario amateur LiDAR finds (earthworks, mill sites) returned only known excavated sites: Cedar Creek Earthworks (Essex County; documented 1930s, excavated 1972 and 2013 with magnetometry and shovel tests, not LiDAR) and Southwold Earthworks (Neutral village site, Parks Canada) — [Wikipedia: Cedar Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Creek_Earthworks_Site); [Wikipedia: Southwold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwold_Earthworks)
- A 2020 Iroquoian village (ca. AD 1300–1600, 25 structural features, 20 longhouse post molds) near Kitchener was found by road-construction excavation, not remote sensing — [Archaeology Magazine](https://www.archaeology.org/news/9146-201016-canada-ontario-village); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/remnants-iroquois-woodland-village-discovered-ontario-180976099/)
- Searches for "LiDAR + Iroquoian village detection Ontario" returned only village-layout and longhouse-orientation studies based on excavation plans, plus forestry LiDAR papers — [Ontario Archaeology (Norcliffe)](https://ontarioarchaeology.org/wp-content/uploads/oa023-01_norcliffe.pdf); [Warrick & Dodd book](https://press.uottawa.ca/en/9781772821185/reconstructing-ontario-iroquoian-village-organization-ontario-iroquois-tradition-longhouses/)
- Searches for War of 1812 earthworks LiDAR in Ontario/Niagara found nothing Ontario-specific; closest is Carleton Island (NY) where "LIDAR is now confirming that some of the Revolutionary War earthworks on Carleton Island still exist" — [Thousand Islands Life](https://www.thousandislandslife.com/BackIssues/Archive/tabid/393/articleType/ArticleView/articleId/2246/Technology-Opens-Window-into-Past.html); Fort York archaeology has been ongoing since the 1970s (method unspecified) — [Archaeology of the War of 1812 (book listing)](https://www.kinokuniya.co.jp/f/dsg-12-EY00101494)
- UK: retired road engineer David Ratledge used open Environment Agency LiDAR to find a 23-mile Roman road between Ribchester and Lancaster after 45 years of searching; the data was "only made freely available to the public in 2013" — [Quartz](https://qz.com/616725/amateur-archaeologists-are-finding-lost-roman-roads-using-lasers); [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/archaeologists-are-using-laser-technology-to-find-long-lost-roman-roads)
- UK: a University of Exeter lockdown project using volunteers on aerial/LiDAR maps found "remnants of more than 20 miles of Roman road, 30 prehistoric or Roman settlements, and 20 prehistoric burial mounds" — [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/amateur-archaeologists-make-discoveries-during-lockdown-180974886/)
- UK cautionary tale: one amateur's claimed Roman road in Kent turned out to be "a massive Welsh gas main" — [Kent Archaeological Society](https://www.kentarchaeology.org.uk/magazine/126/07-lidar-landscape-investigations)
- Netherlands "Heritage Quest": AI paired with volunteers mapped 2,000 km² in about three months; with human verification the project confirmed new sites with ~90% accuracy, and volunteers caught subtle patterns the AI missed — [Lidar News](https://lidarnews.com/citizens-lidar-archaeological-discoveries-in-the-netherlands/)

**AI-assisted LiDAR site detection 2024–2026 (methods now open and retrainable)**
- ADAF (Automatic Detection of Archaeological Features) is an open deep-learning tool trained on Irish LiDAR labelled for enclosures, ringforts and barrows; in a 197 km² real-world test it detected features manual survey had missed; it can be retrained for other regions and monument types — [Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352409X26001689)
- August 2026: researchers with only 12 known circular (kiln) structures in Louisiana's Kisatchie National Forest generated simulated training examples in LiDAR DEMs and found previously unknown features — [Archaeology Magazine](https://archaeologymag.com/2026/08/ai-trained-on-simulated-sites-finds-unknown-features/)
- July 2026: a Nature LiDAR study revealed hidden Amazon earthworks from a civilization of up to 3 million people — [Archaeology Magazine](https://archaeologymag.com/2026/07/hidden-amazon-earthworks-from-lost-civilization/); [HeritageDaily](https://www.heritagedaily.com/2026/07/lidar-survey-reveals-vast-pre-columbian-civilisation/158793)
- Point-cloud deep learning (Mazzacca & Remondino) reached only ~51% structure IoU at one resolution, and higher resolutions cost far more compute — [Lidar News](https://lidarnews.com/lidar-archaeology-ai-point-cloud/)
- OpenAI's Amazon archaeology contest: the winning "Black Bean" team trained models on public LiDAR and satellite data and used GPT-4o to learn site patterns, identifying 67 one-square-mile candidate patches — candidates only, not confirmed sites — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/amazon-openai-competition-archaeology-machine-learning-artificial-intelligence)
- ChatGPT-4o produced usable magnetic-anomaly maps from magnetometer survey data at Sapallitepa (Uzbekistan) "based solely on textual prompts" — [Archaeological Prospection](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/arp.70006); a separate project used ChatGPT to write anomaly-detection code achieving "detection performance comparable to that of human experts" — [Heritage (MDPI)](https://doi.org/10.3390/heritage7100256)
- Great Lakes region: the Sixty Islands site on the Menominee River (Michigan–Wisconsin border) was surveyed by drone LiDAR in May 2023 at the request of Menominee tribal authorities; ridged garden beds 4–12 inches high cover ~330 acres (ten times previous estimates), with only ~40% of the site surveyed; a dance ring, possible trading-post foundation, two logging camps and burial mounds were also detected; published in Science, 2025 — [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/massive-field-where-native-american-farmers-grew-corn-beans-and-squash-1000-years-ago-discovered-in-michigan-180986758/); [Dartmouth](https://fas.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/06/archaeologists-find-intensive-indigenous-farming-michigan); [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/?p=3155680)

**Legal and ethical rules in Ontario**
- The Ontario archaeological sites database is listed in the provincial open-data catalogue as not releasable: "This data is not and will not be made available" because location data would expose sites to looters; it is populated by licensees' Site Record forms — [Ontario Data Catalogue](https://data2.ontario.ca/dataset/ontario-archaeological-sites)
- Under s. 65.1 of the Ontario Heritage Act the ministry keeps a Public Register of Archaeological Reports; any member of the public can request a report, but the ministry removes personal information and site locations under FIPPA — [ontario.ca/archaeology](https://www.ontario.ca/page/archaeology)
- "If you have found an archaeological site or archaeological material, we encourage you to report it and to disturb the site as little as possible. You can send any information you have to archaeology@ontario.ca" — [ontario.ca](https://www.ontario.ca/page/archaeology)
- Any archaeological fieldwork in Ontario requires a licence under the Act (R.R.O. 1990, Reg. 881) — [Ontario e-Laws](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/900881); an Ontario Archaeological Society leaflet cites fines up to $50,000 for individuals and $250,000 for corporations for altering a site or removing artifacts without a licence (figures may be dated) — [OAS Ottawa "Don't Loot"](https://ottawa.ontarioarchaeology.on.ca/Don%27tLoot.pdf)
- Accidental finds versus deliberate collecting: "if you have found something and removed it without knowing what it was, you won't get in trouble. However, if you go out looking to collect archaeological artifacts on purpose, you are breaking the law" — [Museum of Ontario Archaeology](https://archaeologymuseum.ca/stories/think-you-found-an-artifact/)
- Municipalities keep site existence and location confidential under municipal–provincial data-sharing agreements (City of Vaughan policy) — [Vaughan archaeology study, Appendix A](https://www.Vaughan.ca/sites/default/files/10kArchaeology-firstnatiions_study-appendixA.pdf); Region of Waterloo notes site location information "is considered by the MCM to be confidential and/or sensitive information that cannot be made public" — [Region of Waterloo report](https://www.regionofwaterloo.ca/media/nmng0u3f/docs_admin-5372511-v1-p383-0386-2023_13nov2023_sd.pdf)
- Ontario archaeological site forms (for licensees) — [ontario.ca](https://ontario.ca/page/archaeological-site-forms)

### Inferences
- Ontario LiDAR archaeology is an open niche by the evidence available: zero amateur or published LiDAR site-detection finds surfaced for Ontario, while the same workflow (open government LiDAR + hillshade/local-relief visualisation + historical-map cross-check) has repeatedly produced newsworthy amateur finds in the UK and citizen-plus-AI finds in the Netherlands.
- Realistic LiDAR targets in southern Ontario are historic-era and geomorphic, not precontact villages: Ontario Iroquoian villages are known mostly from post-mold plans (no surface earthworks), and true earthworks like Southwold/Cedar Creek are rare, so an amateur should aim at 19th-century mill races, dams, lime kilns, relict road alignments, farmstead foundations, field boundaries and logging/charcoal features in forested ravines, conservation areas and the Oak Ridges Moraine — the same feature classes volunteers found in the UK.
- The ADAF tool and the Kisatchie "simulated training data" approach mean a laptop-scale, retrainable detector is now feasible for an individual; the Heritage Quest 90%-with-human-verification figure is the right expectation for AI-flagged candidates.
- Legal shape of a report: an amateur may analyse open LiDAR freely, may do a "look, don't touch" surface visit, must not dig, probe, metal-detect or collect, should send candidates privately to archaeology@ontario.ca (and, for Toronto, to a licensed archaeologist/heritage body), and must not publish precise coordinates of a suspected precontact or burial site; historic-industrial features (mills, roads) are far less sensitive and are the safest newsworthy category.
- Toronto proper is probably covered by the provincial DTM and/or HRDEM Mosaic (NRCan shows downtown Toronto point cloud), but acquisition year and whether 2019+ flights are included could not be verified; the tile-index ZIP is the first thing to check.
- The Sixty Islands precedent shows the Great Lakes region is under-surveyed for subtle agricultural/earthwork features (40% of one site surveyed; 10x size revision), and that partnership with the relevant First Nation from the start is the accepted model.

### Gaps
- Could not open the Ontario DTM/HRDEM pages to confirm Toronto/GTA acquisition years, resolution per project, or whether 2020–2026 flights are included (fetches blocked).
- Did not find (or could not search for, budget exhausted) any LiDAR-based archaeological work by TRCA, Rouge National Urban Park, Heritage Toronto or the Toronto chapter of the Ontario Archaeological Society; absence here is absence of search results, not confirmed absence of work.

- Burial sites in Ontario are governed by additional legislation (funeral/burial legislation) beyond the Heritage Act; not verified in this research and must be checked before any lost-cemetery work.

## Key question 2: Biodiversity — iNaturalist, eBird, GBIF, Xeno-canto: range extensions, first provincial records, invasive detections, phenology shifts

### Takeaway
Amateur "firsts" via iNaturalist are routine in Ontario (one experienced moth-er estimates ~10 species new to Ontario and Canada per year come through iNaturalist once experts confirm), first-for-Canada records in 2025–2026 include a crayfish, a lichen and (in birds) a Pacific Golden-Plover identified from photos after the fact; the data-mining angle (systematically cross-checking the "needs ID" backlog against provincial checklists with AI triage) and iNaturalist-based phenology analysis for Ontario appear under-exploited, while simply posting observations is crowded.

### Cited Findings
- A Feralia (Substack) author estimates "every year, iNaturalist observations generate about 10 species that are new to both Ontario and Canada, provided an expert confirms the ID"; the author's 2020 Anointed Sallow upload was the first iNaturalist record of that moth for Ontario (normally Missouri to southern Pennsylvania) — [Feralia](https://feralia.substack.com/p/discovering-rare-moths-with-inaturalist)
- June 2025: a provincial arthropod zoologist reported a Paintedhand Mudbug crayfish from Ojibway Prairie Provincial Park, "a species of crayfish never before reported in Canada" — [Ontario Parks blog](https://blog.ontarioparks.ca/inaturalist-at-ontario-parks/)
- Fall 2025: a Pacific Golden-Plover at Oshawa, 20–23 September, "was the first record for Ontario" and was identified only after the bird had left, from photos — [ABA Ontario Fall 2025](https://www.aba.org/ontario-fall-2025/)
- Moths of Northern Ontario (iNaturalist project) 2025 journal: first record of Sharp Angle Shades for Algoma; Bride Underwing new for Northern Ontario; a June 2025 post flags a "possible first record of this species for Canada" for Calosima melanostriatella — [Summer 2025 update](https://www.inaturalist.org/projects/moths-of-northern-ontario/journal/113820-summer-2025-update); [project journal](https://www.inaturalist.org/projects/moths-of-northern-ontario/journal)
- iNaturalist Canada's "Canada's Groundbreaking Observations" project lists a September 2025 Ontario entry described as "Globally rare macrolichen new for Canada" — [iNaturalist Canada](https://www.inaturalist.ca/projects/canada-s-groundbreaking-observations)
- Entomology Today (Nov 2024): "curious people" posting to iNaturalist act as passive surveillance that catches new invasive species — [Entomology Today](https://entomologytoday.org/2024/11/19/curious-people-invasive-species-passive-surveillance-inaturalist/)
- Spotted lanternfly: CFIA confirmed the first detection of a live population-type find in Canada at Windsor, Ontario on September 15, 2026; CFIA-confirmed Ontario observations rose to 17 in 2025 from 7 in 2024, mostly on transport vehicles; CFIA counts iNaturalist/Facebook reports as sightings but "has not always been able to verify them"; a St. Catharines resident's dead specimen uploaded to iNaturalist was logged as an interception — [CFIA observations page](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/plant-health/invasive-species/insects/spotted-lanternfly/observations-canada); [blogTO Sept 2026](https://www.blogto.com/city/2026/09/spotted-lanternfly-ontario-confirmed/); [Farms.com](https://m.farms.com/news/cfia-confirms-observations-of-spotted-lanternfly-are-up-236854.aspx); [Weather Network](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/nature/animals/invasive-spotted-lanternfly-patient-zero-has-first-live-canadian-detection)
- Ontario rare-bird reporting runs through weekly ONTBIRDS Rare Bird Alerts (2025 examples: Gray Kingbird, Hamilton, Oct 4; Crested Caracara, Sudbury; Violet-green Swallow, York; Lazuli Bunting, Thunder Bay under hybrid discussion); none of the digests labels a sighting a first provincial record — [ONTBIRDS Oct 2–9 2025](https://ontbirds.ca/empathy/thread/QCDNWSX5EYQF7DHSXZKGN7E2ZNXE7YR4); [ONTBIRDS July 18–24 2025](https://lists.ctbirding.org/empathy/thread/AF4EEEZFGAMT7IS7DU5AYYYCQYASMYHR)
- Phenology: no iNaturalist-based flowering-shift study for Ontario found; a Thunder Bay study combined field-naturalist records and herbarium vouchers for 11 spring-flowering species against growing degree days — [Canadian Field-Naturalist 135(4), 2021](https://canadianfieldnaturalist.ca/index.php/cfn/article/download/2567/2779/13515); PlantWatch Canada found first-bloom advanced ~9 days for 19 species in 2001–2012 — [Scientific Reports](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/srep02239)
- Phenology methods: a Weibull-based estimator of flowering onset from opportunistic iNaturalist photos, designed around the platform's road/population and in-season biases — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/794396.full.pdf); PhenoVision provides machine annotations of flower/fruit presence for iNaturalist plant photos up to March 2024 — [PhenoVision dataset](https://data.ibercivis.es/datasets/phenovision/); community discussion of shifting phenology annotations — [iNaturalist Forum](https://forum.inaturalist.org/t/shifting-plant-phenology-and-annotations/75174)
- Ontario taxon-group projects exist for grasshoppers/crickets/katydids and moths — [iNaturalist Canada Orthoptera project](https://www.inaturalist.ca/projects/grasshoppers-crickets-and-katydids-of-ontario)

### Inferences
- "First record" discoveries are a numbers game dominated by under-identified taxa (moths, micro-moths, flies, spiders, lichens, fungi, crayfish). A math student with Claude Code can shift the odds by mining rather than observing: pull all Ontario observations (research-grade and needs-ID) through the iNaturalist/GBIF APIs, compare against authoritative provincial checklists, flag taxa with Ontario observations but no checklist entry or with observations far outside the GBIF range envelope, run a vision model for triage, and then recruit taxon experts on iNaturalist to confirm — which is the exact route (expert confirmation) that turns an upload into a GBIF-visible first record.
- The Pacific Golden-Plover case shows retrospective photo re-identification is a legitimate route to a provincial first; an AI pass over Ontario iNaturalist bird photos labelled as common species, looking for overlooked rarities, is a cheap variant.
- Invasive-species early warning is "some competition" (CFIA, Invading Species programmes, Entomology Today documents the pattern) but an automated monitor over unidentified observations for CFIA-regulated pests is still under-built; official confirmation (CFIA) is the recognition route and such detections reliably get CBC/blogTO coverage (spotted lanternfly 2026 is the proof).
- An Ontario/Toronto iNaturalist phenology analysis is an open niche: the methods (Weibull onset estimator, PhenoVision labels) are published and the regional study does not exist in search results; this suits a mathematics student, and the output (a measured advance of first flowering in Toronto over 2012–2026 vs. ECCC temperature) is Varsity/CBC-climate-desk material, but it is a trend finding rather than an "object" discovery.

- Could not open the Feralia post or the Groundbreaking Observations project page; the "~10 species/year" estimate and the lichen entry are from search excerpts only.

- iNaturalist's automatic geoprivacy obscuring of threatened taxa and Ontario's NHIC sensitivity rules were not sourced in this research; they constrain any publication of locations for species at risk.
- Xeno-canto / BirdNET-based Ontario discovery precedents were not searched.

## Key question 3: Satellite imagery — shoreline change, erosion, dumping, heat islands, wetlands, canopy loss

### Takeaway
Open satellite work on Toronto's shoreline and canopy is thin and dated: the only Lake Ontario/GGH shoreline-change study found used Landsat 1990–2011, Scarborough Bluffs recession numbers in circulation come from a 1979 DFO note and a 2016 aerial-photo study, and no citizen Sentinel-2 project on Toronto was found; heat/canopy inequality in Toronto is comparatively crowded (academic, Weather Network, city canopy maps), but a block-level canopy-loss-vs-building-permit analysis exists only for one neighbourhood.

### Cited Findings
- Lisa Bowman's TMU major research paper used Landsat imagery to detect coastline change along Lake Ontario in the Greater Golden Horseshoe from 1990 to 2011, processed in open-source ILWIS — [TMU Spatial Analysis](https://www.torontomu.ca/spatial-analysis/requirements/major-research-paper-abstracts/bowman_lisa/)
- A 1979 DFO technical note measured Scarborough Bluffs erosion at Bluffer's Park (2.41 km reach) at 0.34 m³/m/m/yr from photogrammetric contours — [DFO](https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/Library/79623.pdf); a 2004 geotechnical paper describes the bluffs (>90 m high, Pleistocene deposits) as actively eroding and retreating for centuries — [CGS 2004](https://members.cgs.ca/documents/conference2004/articles/G13.396.pdf)
- A 2016 Trent University study digitised 2005 and 2014 aerial photos and found Lake Ontario bluff toe recession of ~27 cm/yr on average, with "a large amount of uncertainty" in the method — [Trent University](https://batadora.trentu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2022-04/Lake_Ontario_Shoreline_Recession.pdf)
- No Sentinel-2 change-detection work on Toronto, the Portlands or Lake Ontario wetlands surfaced; results were generic coastal/wetland Sentinel-2 methods (e.g., a 2026 BGS shoreline-extraction workflow with cloud masking and land–water classification) — [NERC Open Research Archive](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/542175/)
- Toronto canopy/heat: a study of Toronto postal codes estimates each extra percentage point of tree cover lowers summer land-surface temperature by ~0.05 °C and cuts energy use ~2.5% — [CEPR/VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/cool-cities-value-urban-trees); overlaying the city's canopy map with income data shows wealthier neighbourhoods have more shade; city goal is 40% canopy by 2050 — [The Weather Network](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/nature/habitats/torontos-uneven-tree-canopy-divide-exposed-as-city-plans-for-hotter-future); a study of 544 Toronto neighbourhoods found heat-related ambulance calls far higher where canopy is under 5% — [ASU/Elsevier Pure](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-relationship-between-neighbourhood-tree-canopy-cover-and-heat/)
- Harbord Village residents inventoried all ~4,000 of their trees, then tested whether housing renovation drove tree loss using the City of Toronto's open building-permit data — [Geothink](https://geothink.ca/open-data-and-urban-forests-whats-next/)
- Light pollution: no Toronto VIIRS study found; globally VIIRS-DNB radiance rises ~2.2%/yr while Globe at Night star counts imply ~9.6%/yr, a gap attributed to LED spectral shifts VIIRS misses — [arXiv 2409.13111](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.13111); industrialised countries show VIIRS declines tied to LED switching — [astrobiology.com summary of Kyba et al.](https://astrobiology.com/?p=14503)

### Inferences
- Shoreline: a reach-by-reach, open, 2015–2026 Sentinel-2 plus Landsat (1984–) recession analysis of the Scarborough Bluffs, Toronto Islands and Leslie Street Spit would be the first public update since the 1990–2011 Landsat MRP; at ~0.3 m/yr recession and 10 m pixels, the finding must come from decadal baselines or sub-pixel shoreline extraction, or from differencing two LiDAR epochs (the licensed 2015 city LiDAR vs the open provincial DTM/HRDEM), which is the more powerful route if both epochs can be obtained.
- Canopy: scaling the Harbord Village method citywide (building permits + street-tree inventory + Sentinel-2 NDVI 2016–2026) is a clear "some competition" niche with a strong local-news angle (which wards lost the most canopy to redevelopment); the heat-island-equity story itself is crowded.
- Light pollution: a ward-level VIIRS 2012–2026 trend for Toronto and the GTA, checking for the LED-conversion dip, is an open niche by search evidence; the VIIRS blue-blindness caveat must be addressed, and RASC Toronto Centre / DarkSky are plausible verifiers.
- Illegal dumping and "new wetlands" via satellite: nothing found either way; too thin to rate.

### Gaps
- Toronto's own LiDAR-based canopy rasters (multiple epochs) were not located; availability as open data is unverified.
- No Toronto PurpleAir sensor count or network density was found (see KQ6).
- TRCA Scarborough Waterfront Project monitoring data were not searched.

## Key question 4: City open data — what has been mined, what has not (as of 2026)

### Takeaway
Parking tickets, TTC delays, campaign donations and 311 have been mined heavily and publicly; the ASE speed-camera removal (Nov 2025) is a live, news-saturated natural experiment that still lacks a rigorous public analysis; building permits versus tree loss, noise, and several transportation datasets show no public analyses in search results.

### Cited Findings
- Parking tickets: ~37 million records over 17 years; 4700 Keele Street alone had over 100,000 tickets — [Toronto Open Data blog](https://open.toronto.ca/torontos-1-7b-parking-enforcement-system/); School of Cities map of 2011–2020: 22.8 million tickets, >$1 billion in fines — [School of Cities](https://schoolofcities.github.io/parking-tickets-toronto/); a 2023–2025 geocoded dataset with REST API/MCP tools — [GitHub Nshipyard](https://github.com/Nshipyard/toronto-parking-tickets-geocoded); time-window explorer — [parking-patterns.pages.dev](https://parking-patterns.pages.dev/); bike-lane tickets 17,220 last year, up 54% (FOI, TorontoToday) — [TorontoToday](https://www.torontotoday.ca/local/city-planning-development/must-read-bike-lane-parking-tickets-surge-2025-12038941)

## Key question 5: Seismology, weather, hydrology — quarry blasts, meteors, seiches, water levels

### Takeaway
Quarry blasts outnumber earthquakes roughly 5:1 in eastern Canada and the public blast catalogue is acknowledged to be incomplete, Raspberry Shake precedents exist for Montreal and Oslo but none for Ontario, and Lake Ontario seiche/meteotsunami events are discussed in consultant reports but no Toronto event catalogue was found — all three are plausible open niches with official verifiers (NRCan Earthquakes Canada, ECCC/DFO).

### Cited Findings
- Canada's National Earthquake Database paper (Seismica, 2024) states that in eastern Canada "blast events in this region may outnumber earthquakes by approximately 5:1" and describes the public blast catalogue as analyst-reviewed but incomplete — [Seismica](https://seismica.library.mcgill.ca/article/download/1964/3719/34421); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/5329104a5d3442a7be797e11a0608300)
- NRCan's Stephen Crane: the network "is a very sensitive network, so we can pick up things like mining events and quarry blasts"; a Dec 13, 2019 M2.1 event 14 km north of Wiarton was traced to a quarry off Bruce County Road 9 and "it is unusual for that area for us to pick one up"; blasts start sharply and fade quickly whereas earthquakes build — [CTV London](https://london.ctvnews.ca/that-earthquake-in-wiarton-ont-wasn-t-really-an-earthquake-1.4736929)
- A Raspberry Shake owner in Montreal recorded a limestone-quarry blast logged as M2.1 MN and a small earthquake the same day — [Raspberry Shake community](https://community.raspberryshake.org/t/montreal-urban-seismology-on-a-weekday/3110); a 2024 Seismica study used up to 11 Raspberry Shakes to detect, locate and classify construction blasts in Oslo — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/5329104a5d3442a7be797e11a0608300); close-range blast monitoring needs a 3-component unit plus infrasound — [Raspberry Shake community](https://community.raspberryshake.org/t/usage-question-quarry-explosion-monitoring/2117)
- Raspberry Shake data are served through FDSN as network RASPISHAKE — [FDSN](https://www.fdsn.org/datacenters/detail/RASPISHAKE/); citizen stations are noisier because they sit in residences — [Journal of Seismology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10950-022-10098-5)
- Meteors: the Elginfield Infrasound Array (four sensors, ~20 km north of London, Ontario) operates with the ASGARD all-sky camera network as a regional fireball observatory; a 2025 GSA poster describes 71 events (2006–2011) linking optical and infrasound records and notes only a small fraction of optical meteors are confidently matched to infrasound — [arXiv 1407.6331](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.6331); [GSA Connects 2025](https://gsameetings.secure-platform.com/connects25/solicitations/103002/sessiongallery/schedule/items/95374/application/7631); Raspberry Shake advises cross-referencing suspected meteor signals with fireball databases — [Raspberry Shake blog](https://raspberryshake.org/to-catch-a-meteor/)
- Lake Ontario: TRCA notes seiche can cause 10–20 cm fluctuations and that wave models ignore local mini-seiche between the inner harbour and Toronto Islands and thunderstorm winds — [Port Lands flood protection appendix](https://portlandsto.ca/wp-content/uploads/Appendix_B2-High-Lake-Level-Analysis-2019-Baird-2019b.pdf); Baird defined storm surge from hourly Toronto water levels minus a smoothed static level — [Baird 2019a](https://portlandsto.ca/wp-content/uploads/Appendix_B1-High-Lake-Level-Analysis-2017-Baird-2019a.pdf); NOAA GLERL ERDDAP hosts a Lake Ontario average water level dataset (1992–2022, "still in development") — [GLERL ERDDAP](https://apps.glerl.noaa.gov/erddap/info/LO_average_water_level_2022/index.html); record 2019 levels — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/lake-ontario-record-levels-1.5155962)
- Lake Ontario bathymetry (NOAA) was built from historic soundings plus University of New Brunswick multibeam grids — [data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/bathymetry-of-lake-ontario); shipwreck discoveries are made by private teams with their own multibeam/side-scan (Shannon re-found 2024; HMS Ontario by Kennard/Scoville) and the Lake Ontario National Marine Sanctuary (est. Sept 2024) survey found 17 additional wrecks — [URI OECI](https://web.uri.edu/oeci/about/oeci-projects-main-page/shipwrecks-of-lake-ontario/); [Sketchfab Shannon](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/shannon-7a1e1279411a4b87aa84561eceb5ba0b)
- Crater candidates in Ontario (Charity Shoal in Lake Ontario, Holleford, Franktown, Prince Edward Point) are long-known and investigated with gravity/magnetic/drilling; Franktown drilling found no impact geology — [Crater Explorer](https://craterexplorer.ca/franktown-structure/); [McMaster thesis (Armour 2022)](https://macsphere.mcmaster.ca/handle/11375/27807); [Wikipedia Charity Shoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_Shoal_crater)

### Inferences
- Quarry-blast cataloguing for Ontario is an open niche with an unusually clear verifier: an amateur can pull open Earthquakes Canada event lists and FDSN waveforms (CN network plus RASPISHAKE), classify blast-like signals by onset/duration/time-of-day, cluster them to recurring source locations (Milton/Acton/Flamborough escarpment quarries, Bruce County, eastern Ontario), and compare against the acknowledged-incomplete public blast catalogue; NRCan analysts have shown willingness to discuss such events publicly. News angle: "Toronto-area quarries shake seismometers N times a week" is local-paper material; misclassified "earthquakes" (Wiarton 2019) are the headline-grade catch.
- A Toronto-gauge seiche/meteotsunami catalogue (hourly or finer DFO/CHS water levels vs ECCC radar/pressure) is plausible and un-done by search evidence, but Great Lakes meteotsunami literature exists for other lakes and the frequency of the Toronto gauge data is unverified.
- Meteor infrasound is poorly suited: Western University's Elginfield array and cameras already cover southwestern Ontario professionally.
- Shipwrecks via open bathymetry and crater-hunting are poor fits: discovery requires own sonar or drilling/shock-metamorphism evidence, and crater-candidate hunting is a crowded hobby.

### Gaps
- Number and location of Raspberry Shake stations in Ontario/Toronto not verified (station map not fetched).
- Great Lakes meteotsunami climatology papers (which I recall exist, e.g., for Lake Michigan) were not retrieved; the Toronto-specific gap is therefore "not found," not "known absent."
- Sampling frequency of the Toronto water-level gauge and whether ECCC/DFO serve it openly at sub-hourly resolution not verified.

## Key question 9 (objective): Vetted shortlist of candidate projects

### Takeaway
Twelve projects below fit a laptop, Claude Code, free data and weeks-to-months of effort; the five strongest on the combined "open niche + official verifier + local news value" test are (1) Ontario LiDAR historic-landscape survey of GTA ravines/moraine, (7) Ontario quarry-blast catalogue, (3) iNaturalist "needs-ID" first-record mining, (10) Toronto lost-burial-grounds georeferenced map, and (8) Toronto VIIRS light-pollution trend.

### Cited Findings
- Data sources, precedents and legal rules for every project are cited in KQ1–KQ8 above; project-specific anchors are linked inline below.

### Inferences
#### Project 1 — LiDAR survey of Toronto-region ravines, conservation areas and the Oak Ridges Moraine for undocumented historic-era features (mill races/dams, lime kilns, lost road alignments, farmstead foundations, relict field boundaries)
- Discovery: specific, previously unrecorded 19th-century landscape features, each cross-checked against Tremaine 1860 / county atlases / Goad / 1947 aerials to show it is absent from modern records.
- Data: Ontario DTM (Lidar-Derived) 1 km tiles and tile index ([data.ontario.ca](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/ontario-digital-terrain-model-lidar-derived); [tile index ZIP](https://www.publicdocs.mnr.gov.on.ca/mirb/OntarioDTM_LidarDerived_TileIndex.zip)); Ontario Classified Point Cloud LAZ ([data2.ontario.ca](https://data2.ontario.ca/dataset/ontario-classified-point-cloud-lidar-derived)); NRCan HRDEM/HRDEM Mosaic ([NRCan](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/science-data/science-research/geomatics/more-high-resolution-lidar-elevation-data-now-available); [OpenTopography](https://opentopography.org/node/3589)); historical maps ([Historical Maps of Toronto](https://activehistory.ca/blog/2013/04/08/historical-maps-of-toronto-a-collection-of-maps-to-amuse-delight-and-inform/); [McMaster Goad 1910](https://library.mcmaster.ca/maps/geospatial/city-toronto-fire-insurance-plans-1910-georeferenced)); 1947 aerials ([U of T MDL](https://mdl-data.library.utoronto.ca/items/show/1137)).
- Method: download tiles for a few hundred km² (ravine corridors of the Humber, Don, Rouge, Credit, Duffins; Oak Ridges Moraine forests); compute multi-directional hillshade, local relief model and sky-view factor in Python; optionally retrain ADAF or a small U-Net with simulated targets (Kisatchie approach) for linear banks, rectangular platforms and circular pits; rank candidates; eliminate pipelines, trails and modern grading (the Kent "gas main" lesson) using modern basemaps; cross-reference historical maps; produce a private candidate dossier.
- Confirmation: send dossier privately to archaeology@ontario.ca ([ontario.ca](https://www.ontario.ca/page/archaeology)); approach the Ontario Archaeological Society (Toronto chapter), TRCA/Rouge NUP heritage staff or a U of T/York archaeologist for a licensed walkover; "look don't touch" visit permitted; no digging, probing or collecting (Reg. 881; fines cited by OAS).
- Effort: 4–10 weeks part-time.
- Saturation: open niche in Ontario — no amateur or academic Ontario LiDAR site detection found across five searches; UK/NL precedents prove the workflow and the news value ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/amateur-archaeologists-make-discoveries-during-lockdown-180974886/); [Lidar News](https://lidarnews.com/citizens-lidar-archaeological-discoveries-in-the-netherlands/)).
- Main risk: false positives; site-location confidentiality prevents publishing coordinates of any precontact or burial feature (historic mills/roads are the publishable class); Toronto DTM acquisition year unverified.

#### Project 2 — Glacial landform inventory (DeGeer moraines, iceberg scours, megagrooves, crevasse-squeeze ridges) in LiDAR-covered southern Ontario sheets not yet mapped
- Discovery: new landform fields extending or contradicting the Bukhari 2024 / Mulligan 2019 ice-flow reconstructions.
- Data: same Ontario DTM/HRDEM tiles; OGS/GSC field guide (Eyles et al. 2018) — [GSC Open File 8481](https://download-telecharger.services.geo.ca/pub/nrcan_rncan/publications/STPublications_PublicationsST/313/313040/of_8481.pdf).
- Method: automated ridge/furrow detection (orientation statistics, spacing) across tiles; flow-set clustering; compare with published maps.
- Confirmation: OGS Quaternary geologists or UTSC (Nick Eyles group) — [UTSC news](https://planetearth.utsc.utoronto.ca/news); a note in a Canadian geoscience journal.
- Effort: 6–12 weeks. Saturation: some competition (active academic/OGS program) but vast unmapped area. Risk: needs geomorphology reading to avoid misclassifying anthropogenic ridges; news value is university-paper rather than Star.

#### Project 3 — Mining the iNaturalist "needs ID" backlog for first Ontario/Canada records in under-identified taxa (moths, flies, spiders, lichens, fungi, crayfish)
- Discovery: species with Ontario observations absent from provincial/national checklists, confirmed to research grade and exported to GBIF.

- Confirmation: expert ID → research grade → GBIF; short note in Canadian Field-Naturalist or Ontario Insects; CBC covers Canada firsts ([Ontario Parks Mudbug](https://blog.ontarioparks.ca/inaturalist-at-ontario-parks/)).

Saturation: some competition (iNat curators, "Groundbreaking Observations" project; ~10 firsts/year already flow through — [Feralia](https://feralia.substack.com/p/discovering-rare-moths-with-inaturalist)); the systematic-mining angle appears open. Risk: expert bandwidth; misID; geoprivacy for species at risk.

#### Project 5 — Toronto flowering/leaf-out phenology shift from iNaturalist photos (2012–2026) vs ECCC climate data

Saturation: open niche (no Ontario iNat phenology study found; PlantWatch 2001–2012 is the last Canadian citizen result — [Sci Rep](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/srep02239)). #### Project 6 — Scarborough Bluffs / Toronto Islands / Leslie Spit shoreline change, 1984–2026, from open Landsat/Sentinel-2 (and LiDAR differencing if two epochs obtainable)
- Discovery: first public reach-by-reach recession rates since the 1990–2011 Landsat MRP ([TMU](https://www.torontomu.ca/spatial-analysis/requirements/major-research-paper-abstracts/bowman_lisa/)); hotspots exceeding the ~27 cm/yr regional figure ([Trent 2016](https://batadora.trentu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2022-04/Lake_Ontario_Shoreline_Recession.pdf)).

- Method: water-index shoreline extraction per cloud-free scene; transect-based rates; DEM-of-difference for the bluff crest where two LiDAR epochs exist.
- Confirmation: TRCA (Scarborough Waterfront Project) and the 2004 CGS authors' community ([CGS 2004](https://members.cgs.ca/documents/conference2004/articles/G13.396.pdf)). Effort: 4–8 weeks. Saturation: some competition (consultants do this internally; no recent open study). Risk: 10 m pixels vs 0.3 m/yr change; needs long baseline or sub-pixel methods.

#### Project 7 — Ontario quarry-blast catalogue from open seismic data (Earthquakes Canada + Raspberry Shake)
- Discovery: recurring blast sources, time-of-day/weekday patterns, blasts missing from the public catalogue, and any "earthquakes" that are actually blasts (cf. Wiarton 2019 — [CTV](https://london.ctvnews.ca/that-earthquake-in-wiarton-ont-wasn-t-really-an-earthquake-1.4736929)).
- Data: NEDB event lists and FDSN waveforms (CN network); RASPISHAKE via FDSN ([FDSN](https://www.fdsn.org/datacenters/detail/RASPISHAKE/)); methods from Oslo ([Seismica 2024](https://doaj.org/article/5329104a5d3442a7be797e11a0608300)).
- Method: ObsPy pipeline; STA/LTA detection; blast-vs-earthquake classifier on onset sharpness, duration, spectral ratio, daytime weekday occurrence; location by arrival times; cluster to quarries.
- Confirmation: Earthquakes Canada analysts (NRCan) who already review blasts; the NEDB authors acknowledge incompleteness ([Seismica](https://seismica.library.mcgill.ca/article/download/1964/3719/34421)).
- Effort: 4–8 weeks. Saturation: open niche for Ontario (Montreal/Oslo precedents only). Risk: Ontario Raspberry Shake density unverified; a blast-pattern story is modest news unless a misclassified event is found.

#### Non-local open niches worth noting
- Great Lakes states' public 1 m LiDAR (USGS 3DEP; not verified here) plus the Sixty Islands precedent (only ~40% of one site surveyed — [Dartmouth](https://fas.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/06/archaeologists-find-intensive-indigenous-farming-michigan)) suggests ridged-field and garden-bed detection across Michigan/Wisconsin is under-surveyed, but Indigenous partnership is mandatory and verification is field-based.
